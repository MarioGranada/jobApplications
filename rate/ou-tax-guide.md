# Munbuh Corp OÜ: taxes, percentages and how to work out your money

_Estonian rules for 2026 (assumed unchanged in 2027). All examples use €600/day × 229 days = €137,400/year (€11,450/month)._

---

## 0. In one minute: which taxes am I liable for?

**Rule: Munbuh pays no tax for earning money. Tax only appears when money leaves the company towards you.**

| What happens | Tax | Who pays | €1,000 example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. A client pays Munbuh | **None** | — | €1,000 sits in Munbuh |
| 2. Money stays in Munbuh | **None** (for as long as it stays) | — | €1,000 still there |
| 3. Munbuh pays business costs (laptop, accountant…) | **None** | — | €1,000 of tools, €0 tax |
| 4. Munbuh pays you a **salary** (board fee) | **33% social tax** on top of gross, then **2% II pillar** + **22% income tax** above €700/month. **No Töötukassa unemployment insurance** on board fees (0.8% + 1.6% applies only to an employment contract) | Company + you | €1,000 → **€728.74 net** to you, €271.26 tax and contributions |
| 5. Munbuh pays you a **dividend** | **22%** of the money taken out | Company | €1,000 → **€780 net** to you, €220 tax |
| 6. ❌ Munbuh pays your **personal** spending | **~41%** (fringe benefit tax) | Company | €1,000 of groceries costs Munbuh €1,705 |
| (+) Client is Estonian and you're VAT-registered | **24% VAT** added on top of the invoice | Client pays it, you pass it on | Invoice €1,240 → €240 goes to the state |

**That's the whole list.** There is no tax on revenue, no yearly profit tax, and no personal tax on dividends.

So "what tax do I pay?" depends on **how you take the money out**:
- **Salary:** ~27% of what the company spends goes to taxes and contributions, and most of it (26%) is your own pension and health insurance.
- **Dividend:** 22%, but no pension or health insurance.
- **Keep it in the company:** 0% for now.

## 1. The taxes that touch your OÜ

| Tax | Rate | Charged on | When | Paid by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Corporate income tax (CIT)** | **22/78 of the net dividend** (= 28.2% on top of what you receive = **22% of the profit paid out**) | Profit **distributed** (dividends, hidden distributions) | Month after the payout (TSD by the 10th) | Company |
| CIT on retained profit | **0%** | Profit kept in the company | Never, until paid out | — |
| Personal tax on dividends | **0%** | Dividends already taxed at 22/78 | — | — |
| **Social tax** | **33%** (20% pension + 13% health) | Board fee or salary, **on top** of gross | Monthly (TSD by the 10th) | Company |
| Unemployment insurance | 0.8% + 1.6% | **Only salaries under an employment contract.** Board fees are exempt. | Monthly | Company + you |
| **II pillar pension** | 2% (or 4/6% if you chose) | Your gross fee or salary | Monthly | You (withheld) |
| **Income tax** | **22%** after the **€700/month tax-free allowance** | Fee or salary − II pillar − €700 | Monthly | You (withheld) |
| **VAT** | **24%** | Your invoices (only if the client is Estonian; reverse charge for EU clients) | KMD by the 20th of the next month | Collected from the client, passed to the state |

**Three key facts:**
1. **Money inside the company is not taxed.** Tax only happens when money moves to you.
2. **There are only two clean ways to move money to you:** board fee or salary (social tax + income tax), and dividends (22/78 CIT, nothing else).
3. **VAT is never income.** It's money you hold for the state, so set it aside in a separate account.

## 2. What one euro of company money becomes in your pocket

This is the most useful table. Each route has a different conversion rate.

| Route | Calculation | **You keep per €1 the company spends** | Lost to tax |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dividend** | €1 − 22% CIT | **€0.78** | 22% |
| Board fee, within the €700 allowance | (1 − 2% II pillar) ÷ 1.33 | €0.74 | 26% |
| Board fee, above €700 | (1 − 2% − 22% × 0.98) ÷ 1.33 | **€0.57** | 43% |
| Employment-contract salary, above €700 | (1 − 1.6% − 2% − 22% × 0.964) ÷ 1.338 | €0.56 | 44% |
| Company pays a personal expense (fringe benefit) | 1 ÷ (1.282 × 1.33) | €0.59 | 41% |
| **Genuine business expense** (laptop, software…) | Fully deductible, no CIT, VAT reclaimable | **€1.24 of goods** (vs €0.78 cash as a dividend; see §6) | 0% |

**Why the €946 board fee exists anyway:** it's slightly less efficient than a dividend (€0.74–0.57 vs €0.78), but it is what gives you **health insurance** (social tax must be paid on at least the minimum monthly base) and some **state pension**. The extra cost is small. Everything above that is cheapest as dividends.

## 3. Simple example: a client pays Munbuh €1,000

> **Why other sections say €11,450/month:** €600/day × 229 billable days = €137,400/year, and ÷ 12 = €11,450. It's a **yearly average**: a fully worked month (~21 days) brings ~€12,600, and a month with 2 weeks of holiday ~€6,600. The €1,000 below keeps the maths easy. Everything scales in proportion.

### Step 1: the money arrives

```
Client pays invoice ──▶ Munbuh Corp OÜ bank account
   €1,000 (EU client, reverse charge: no VAT)
   €1,240 (Estonian client: €1,000 + €240 VAT)
```

**The €240 VAT is not yours.** Put it aside and pay it to EMTA by the 20th of the next month. From here on we work with **€1,000**.

**At this moment no income tax is due.** Estonian companies don't pay tax on profit they earn, only on profit they **pay out**.

### Step 2: what you can do with each euro

| Option | What happens to €1,000 | Tax now | **Ends up with you** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Keep it in Munbuh** | Stays in the company account. Can sit in a savings deposit or be invested in the company's name (ETFs, deposits) | **€0** (deferred, not erased) | €1,000 still in the company, yours to decide later |
| **B. Spend it on business costs** | e.g. laptop, software, accountant, courses, coworking, liability insurance | **€0** (deductible), and you reclaim the VAT you paid | €1,000 of tools or services you'd otherwise buy yourself |
| **C. Pay yourself a dividend** | €1,000 of profit is distributed | €220 CIT (22%) | **€780** in your personal account, no further tax |
| **D. Pay yourself a board fee** | Company spends €1,000: €751.88 gross fee + €248.12 social tax (33%) | €248 social tax + €15 II pillar + €8 income tax | **€729** (only this good while within the €700 tax-free allowance; above it, ~€570) |
| **E. ❌ Pay personal stuff from the company** | groceries, family trip, private rent, cash taken without paperwork | Counts as a fringe benefit or hidden distribution: about €705 extra tax per €1,000 spent | €1,000 of stuff, but it cost the company **€1,705** |

**In plain words:**
- ✅ **Can use, tax-free:** genuine **business expenses**. Never taxed, because they reduce profit.
- ✅ **Can preserve, not taxed yet:** anything you **leave in the company**. No tax while it stays there, for years if you like. The 22% only applies on the day it's paid out to you.
- ✅ **Can take out, cheaply:** **dividends**, which leave you €780 per €1,000, once allowed (after the annual report is approved).
- ⚠️ **Can take out, more expensively:** **board fee**. €1,000 of fee costs the company €1,330. You need at least the minimum (€946/month) to get health insurance.
- ❌ **Can't:** use the company card or account for personal spending, or move money to yourself without a fee or dividend decision. That's taxed harder than anything else.

### Step 3: a realistic mix of the €1,000

| | Amount | Tax | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Money in | €1,000 | | |
| Accountant (share for the month) | −€80 | €0 | Deductible |
| Software, laptop savings, internet share | −€120 | €0 | Deductible, VAT reclaimed |
| **= Profit left** | **€800** | | |
| ↳ **Option 1: keep it** (buffer for gaps between contracts) | €800 in Munbuh | **€0 now** | 22% only if and when paid out later |
| ↳ **Option 2: pay it out as a dividend** | **€624** to you | €176 CIT | €800 × 78% |

So from €1,000 of revenue you get **€200 of things your work needs (tax-free)** plus either **€800 kept untaxed** or **€624 in your pocket**.

### Scaling up: the same logic at €600/day (€11,450/month average)

```
€11,450  revenue
  −  €200  business costs                 → tax-free (option B)
  −€1,258  minimum board fee + social tax → buys health insurance + €877 net to you (option D)
= €9,992  profit
           ├─ keep it in Munbuh  → €0 tax now (option A), or
           └─ pay as dividend    → €2,198 CIT, €7,794 to you (option C)

Net to you if everything is paid out: €7,794 + €877 = €8,671 (75.7%)
```

Effective total tax ≈ **22.5% of revenue**, and every extra euro billed reaches you as €0.78. Compare that with 42.5% on payroll and 41.2% as an FIE.

## 3b. Paying yourself a monthly "salary" (board fee) to live on

A board fee is your monthly salary from Munbuh. It's the only way to get **health insurance, state pension (I pillar) and II pillar** contributions while also paying rent and groceries. Dividends give you none of those.

### Breakdown: €1,000 gross board fee per month

Read from the company's cost down to your bank account, like the Coursy table:

| # | Step | Formula | Monthly | Yearly | Who receives it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cost to Munbuh** | €1,000 × 1.33 | **€1,330.00** | **€15,960** | |
| 2 | − Social tax: pension part | €1,000 × 20% | −€200.00 | −€2,400 | **Your state pension (I pillar)** |
| 3 | − Social tax: health part | €1,000 × 13% | −€130.00 | −€1,560 | **Your health insurance** (Haigekassa) |
| 4 | **= Gross board fee** | | **€1,000.00** | **€12,000** | |
| 5 | − II pillar 2% | €1,000 × 2% | −€20.00 | −€240 | **Your own pension fund** |
| 6 | − Income tax | (€1,000 − €20 − €700) × 22% = €280 × 22% | −€61.60 | −€739 | State |
| 7 | **= Net to your bank account** | | **€918.40** | **€11,021** | You |

- Of the €1,330 the company spends, **€350 goes to your own social protection** (€200 state pension + €130 health + €20 II pillar). Only €61.60 is "pure" tax.
- No unemployment insurance is charged on board fees, so this doesn't build Töötukassa cover.
- **To receive exactly €1,000 net**, the gross fee is **€1,106.75**. It costs the company €1,471.98: social tax €365.23, II pillar €22.14, income tax €84.62.

### Is €1,000 enough to live on? Probably not in Tallinn

A rough monthly budget for one person in Tallinn (estimate, adjust to your reality):

| Item | ~€/month |
|---|---|
| Rent (1-bedroom) | 700–900 |
| Utilities + internet + phone | 150–250 |
| Groceries | 350–450 |
| Transport (Tallinn public transport is free for registered residents) | 0–80 |
| Clothes, leisure, eating out, gym, other | 300–500 |
| **Total** | **≈ €1,500–2,200** |

### What net salary costs the company, and what it does to your total at €600/day

| Net salary you want | Gross board fee | **Cost to Munbuh** | Your pension + health contributions | Income tax | State pension built per year | **Total net/month** (fee + dividends at €600/day) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| €877 (minimum fee €946) | €946 | €1,258 | €331 | €50 | €2,270 | **€8,671** |
| €918 (fee €1,000) | €1,000 | €1,330 | €350 | €62 | €2,400 | €8,656 |
| €1,000 | €1,107 | €1,472 | €387 | €85 | €2,656 | €8,627 |
| **€2,000** | €2,415 | €3,212 | €845 | €367 | €5,796 | **€8,270** |
| **€2,500** | €3,069 | €4,082 | €1,074 | €508 | €7,366 | **€8,091** |
| €3,000 | €3,723 | €4,952 | €1,303 | €649 | €8,936 | €7,913 |
| €4,064 (same net as Coursy) | €5,115 | €6,802 | €1,790 | €949 | €12,275 | €7,533 |

"Your pension + health contributions" = 20% state pension + 13% health + 2% II pillar.

**How to read it:** the salary is paid every month, so you live on it. The rest of the profit becomes dividends, normally paid once a year after the annual report. The last column is your total over a year, divided by 12.

### Suggested setup
- **Net salary of €2,000–2,500/month** (gross fee €2,415–3,069). That covers a real-life budget with some margin, builds 2.5–3× more state pension than the minimum fee, and includes full health insurance.
- It costs only **€400–580/month of total net** compared with the minimum fee, and **removes the 2027 cash-flow problem** of waiting for dividends.
- Everything else stays in Munbuh as untaxed buffer, or goes out once a year as dividends.

**Paperwork:** record the fee in a written board or shareholder decision (amount, start date). The accountant files the TSD and pays social tax, II pillar and income tax by the **10th of the following month**. Your net arrives in your personal account like a normal salary.

## 3c. A client contract that pays Munbuh €1,000/month: where every euro goes

Scenario: a client pays Munbuh **€1,000/month** (excl. VAT). You want a salary to live on **plus** social security and pension. So all €1,000 is paid out as your **board fee** (salary). For simplicity there are no company costs. See the notes at the end.

### Breakdown

| # | Step | Formula | Monthly | **% of €1,000** | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Client pays Munbuh** | | **€1,000.00** | **100%** | |
| 2 | − Social tax, pension part (20% of gross) | €751.88 × 20% | −€150.38 | **15.0%** | Your state pension (I pillar) |
| 3 | − Social tax, health part (13% of gross) | €751.88 × 13% | −€97.74 | **9.8%** | Health insurance (Haigekassa) |
| 4 | **= Gross salary** | €1,000 ÷ 1.33 | **€751.88** | 75.2% | |
| 5 | − II pillar (2% of gross) | €751.88 × 2% | −€15.04 | **1.5%** | Your own pension fund |
| 6 | − Income tax | (€751.88 − €15.04 − €700) × 22% = €36.84 × 22% | −€8.10 | **0.8%** | State |
| 7 | **= Net salary to you** | | **€728.74** | **72.9%** | You |

### The percentages in one view

```
€1,000 from the client
├── 72.9%  €728.74  net salary in your bank account
├── 15.0%  €150.38  your state pension (I pillar)
├──  9.8%   €97.74  health insurance
├──  1.5%   €15.04  your II pillar pension fund
└──  0.8%    €8.10  income tax
```

- **Pure tax is only 0.8%.** That's because your first €700/month of income is tax-free (the basic allowance).
- **26.3% goes to your own social protection:** pension (15.0% + 1.5%) and health (9.8%).
- **72.9% is cash for rent and groceries.**

### Compared with taking the €1,000 as a dividend instead

| | As salary (board fee) | As dividend |
|---|---|---|
| Net to you | €728.74 (72.9%) | €780.00 (78.0%) |
| Tax | €8.10 income tax (0.8%) | €220.00 corporate tax (22.0%) |
| Pension (I + II pillar) | €165.42 (16.5%) | €0 |
| Health insurance contribution | €97.74 (9.8%) | €0 |
| When you can get it | Every month | After the annual report (once a year) |

At this income level salary is clearly better. You give up €51/month of cash, and in return €263 goes to your pension and health, paid monthly.

### Important notes for the €1,000 scenario
1. **Health insurance:** a board member is normally only health-insured if social tax is paid on at least the **monthly minimum base (€886 in 2026, assumed €946 in 2027)**. €751.88 gross is below that, so you would probably **not** be covered by Haigekassa. To get coverage, the gross fee must be ≥ €946, which costs Munbuh **€1,258/month**. In practice the contract needs to pay at least about €1,260/month, or you top up from retained profit. Check with Haigekassa or your accountant.
2. **Company costs are real.** An accountant (~€50–80/month) and bank fees come out first. With €80 of costs, the salary is paid from €920 (gross €691.73, net €677.90). Income tax drops to €0, because the gross minus II pillar is under the €700 allowance.
3. **VAT:** €12,000/year is under the €40,000 registration threshold, so no VAT registration is needed unless the client is Estonian and you choose to register.
4. **No unemployment insurance** is paid on board fees, so this does not build Töötukassa cover.

## 4. Shortcuts for working out your numbers

With the fee at €946 and costs of €200/month, everything reduces to one line:

> **Monthly net ≈ 78% × monthly revenue − €260**
>
> **Monthly net ≈ day rate × 14.9 − €260**   (14.9 = 229 days × 0.78 ÷ 12)

| Day rate | Monthly revenue | **Monthly net** (shortcut) |
|---|---|---|
| €450 | €8,588 | €6,438 |
| €500 | €9,542 | €7,182 |
| €550 | €10,496 | €7,926 |
| €600 | €11,450 | €8,671 |
| €650 | €12,404 | €9,415 |

- **Each €1 on your day rate adds about €14.90/month net.** €50/day is worth about €745/month.
- **Reverse it to find the rate you need:** day rate = (target monthly net + €260) ÷ 14.9.
  - For €6,000/month: (6,000 + 260) ÷ 14.9 = **€420/day**
  - For €8,000/month: (8,000 + 260) ÷ 14.9 = **€554/day**
- **Hourly:** divide the day rate by 8.
- **Fewer billable days** (holidays, sick days, gaps): every 10 days not billed at €600 costs €6,000 revenue, or about €4,680 net.

**Where the −€260 comes from:** the fee block and costs (€1,258 + €200 = €1,458) come out before the 78%, which removes €1,458 × 0.78 = €1,137 of dividends. But €877 of it comes back to you as the net fee. −€1,137 + €877 = **−€260**. It's a fixed amount, so the bigger your revenue, the closer you get to 78%.

## 5. Choosing the board fee: net now vs pension later

At €600/day:

| Monthly board fee | Net fee/mo | Dividends/mo | **Total net/mo** | Net/yr | State pension (I pillar) accrual/yr |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **€946 (minimum)** | €877 | €7,794 | **€8,671** | €104,049 | €2,270 |
| €2,000 | €1,683 | €6,700 | €8,383 | €100,596 | €4,800 |
| €3,000 | €2,447 | €5,663 | €8,110 | €97,320 | €7,200 |
| €5,000 (same as Coursy) | €3,976 | €3,588 | €7,564 | €90,768 | €12,000 |
| Everything as fee (~€8,460) | ~€6,620 | €0 | €6,620 | ~€79,400 | ~€20,300 |

- **Each €1,000/month of extra fee costs you about €275/month net** and adds €2,400/year to your state pension accrual.
- The usual setup is the **minimum fee**, investing the difference yourself (III pillar, index funds). €275/month invested will very likely beat the state pension it would have bought.
- **A higher fee can make sense in 2027**, because dividends only come after the annual report (see §7). It gives you monthly cash flow, at the cost of ~€275 per €1,000.

## 6. Expenses: the one place where the company beats your pocket

Example: a €1,240 laptop (€1,000 + €240 VAT).

| Who pays | What it really costs |
|---|---|
| **Munbuh pays** (VAT-registered, business use) | €1,000. The €240 VAT is reclaimed and the €1,000 reduces profit, so no CIT is paid on it. **Company money spent: €1,000.** |
| **You pay personally** from dividends | You need €1,240 in your pocket, which takes €1,240 ÷ 0.78 = **€1,590 of company profit**. |

**So €1 of genuine business expense paid by the company is worth about €1.59 of company profit.** Do put real business costs through Munbuh: laptop, monitor, phone (business share), software, courses, conferences, coworking, liability insurance, accountant, and a documented share of home internet and utilities.

**Don't** put personal spending through the company (groceries, family holidays, a private car). That counts as a fringe benefit or hidden distribution and costs more tax than a dividend.

## 6b. VAT reclaim: how it actually works

**Precondition: Munbuh must be VAT-registered.** If it isn't, there's no VAT reclaim at all, and the full price incl. VAT is simply the business cost. At €600/day you'll be above the €40,000 threshold, so you'll register.

**You don't "apply" per purchase.** Each month:

```
VAT you charged clients (output VAT)         e.g. €0 (EU client, reverse charge)
− VAT on your business purchases (input VAT)  e.g. €240 (laptop)
= KMD return by the 20th of next month  →  negative = EMTA refunds €240 to Munbuh
```

Your job is to make sure every business purchase has an **invoice in Munbuh's name** and that it reaches the accountant or accounting software (photo or PDF).

| Case | What you need | VAT reclaimable? |
|---|---|---|
| **Laptop paid with Munbuh's card** | Invoice with **Munbuh Corp OÜ + reg. code (+ VAT no.)**. Ask the shop for a company invoice ("arve firmale") | ✅ €240 of €1,240 |
| **Laptop paid with your personal card** | Same company invoice + an **expense report** (kuluaruanne). Munbuh reimburses you the **full €1,240** | ✅ Munbuh still reclaims the €240, so its real cost is €1,000 and you're out €0 |
| Small purchase ≤ €160 with only a till receipt | A simplified invoice/receipt with the seller's VAT details | ✅ Usually OK |
| Purchase > €160 with only a personal receipt (no company name) | — | ❌ Expense yes, VAT reclaim no |
| **Coffee with a client** (hospitality) | Receipt + note of who and why | ❌ **VAT on hospitality isn't reclaimable** in Estonia. The cost counts toward the €50/month + 2% limit |
| Coffee alone | — | ❌ Personal: don't put it through the company |
| Item also used privately (e.g. a phone) | Business-use share | ⚠️ Only the business share |

What matters is **whose name is on the invoice**, not whose card paid.

## 7. Practical money management: the "bucket" system

Open 3–4 sub-accounts in Munbuh's bank (LHV and Wise both support this). For every payment received:

| Bucket | How much | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **VAT** | 100% of any VAT on the invoice (24% if the agency is Estonian; 0 under reverse charge) | Not yours. Pay it with the KMD by the 20th. |
| 2. **Fixed monthly block** | €1,258 (fee €946 + social tax €312) + costs ~€200 | Pays you monthly and covers health insurance. |
| 3. **Tax reserve** | **22% of what's left** | Future CIT on dividends. Never spend it. |
| 4. **Your money** | The remaining 78% | Yours net. Pay it out as dividends once allowed, or keep it as a buffer. |

Example with an €11,450 payment:
- VAT: €0 (EU agency).
- Fixed block: €1,458.
- Remaining €9,992: €2,198 goes to the tax reserve and **€7,794 is yours.**

**Buffer target:** keep **3–6 months of your personal spending** inside the company as untaxed retained profit. It's your bench-time insurance between contracts. You only pay the 22% when you actually take it out.

### When can dividends actually be paid?
- An OÜ normally distributes profit **based on its approved annual report**. The 2027 report is due by **30 June 2028**, so 2027 profit can usually be paid out from **mid-2028**.
- **Profit Munbuh already retained from previous years** can be distributed as soon as it's in an approved annual report.
- **Monthly dividends:** yes, but only from **profit already in an approved annual report**. The shareholder decision can set a payment schedule (e.g. "€X dividend, paid in 12 monthly instalments"). CIT is declared on the TSD and paid by the 10th of the month after each instalment. Profit earned this year can't be paid out monthly as you go. The share capital must also be fully paid in before any dividend.
- **Bridging 2027:** use a higher board fee for some months (§5), existing retained earnings, or your personal savings. Ask your accountant about this **before the first invoice**. It's the most important practical decision.

## 7b. Why keep money in Munbuh, and how to make it grow

### The point of retained money
1. **Dividends are delayed, not lost.** After year 1 it becomes a rolling cycle: each year you pay yourself (even monthly) from the previous year's profit. The wait only hurts once, in the first year.
2. **Bench-time insurance.** Between contracts, Munbuh keeps paying your board fee from its reserve. As an owner you have no unemployment cover, so this reserve is your cover.
3. **Your money stays untaxed while it waits.** €1,000 in Munbuh can be invested as the full €1,000. Paid out first, only €780 would be left to invest. The 22% is only paid when you finally take it out.
4. **You choose when to take it out**, e.g. in a year with lower income or when you actually need it.

### Legal ways to make it grow (in the company's name)

| Option | Risk | Liquidity | Good for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Business savings account or term deposit** (Estonian banks: LHV, Swedbank, SEB, Luminor, Bigbank, Inbank, Coop…) | Very low. Company deposits are covered by the deposit guarantee up to €100k per bank | Instant (savings) or locked 3–12 months (term) | Tax reserve, VAT, 3–6 months buffer |
| **Money market fund** (via a company investment account at LHV, Lightyear, Interactive Brokers…) | Very low | 1–3 days | Same as above, often a slightly better rate than deposits |
| **Bond ETFs/funds** | Low–medium | 1–3 days | Money you won't need for 1–3 years |
| **Global stock index ETFs** (prefer *accumulating* UCITS ETFs, e.g. Ireland-domiciled) | Higher in the short term, historically beats inflation over 5+ years | 1–3 days | Long-term surplus you won't need soon |

Compare current rates when you open them. Deposit rates move with the ECB rate.

**Tax inside the company:** interest, fund gains and investment income earned by Munbuh are **not taxed while they stay in Munbuh**. Everything is taxed once, at 22%, when paid out to you.

### Example: €10,000 for 10 years at 5%/year

| | Invest inside Munbuh | Pay out first, invest personally (investment account) |
|---|---|---|
| Start | €10,000 | €7,800 (after 22% CIT) |
| After 10 years | €16,289 | €12,705 |
| Tax when you take it out | 22% CIT on the payout: €3,584 | 22% on the €4,905 gain: €1,079 |
| **You end up with** | **€12,705** | **€11,626** |

Investing inside the company leaves you **~€1,080 more**, because the gains are also untaxed until the end.

### Suggested split
1. **Tax and VAT pot** (CIT reserve 22% + VAT + next TSD): savings account. Never invest it.
2. **Buffer, 3–6 months of costs + board fee:** savings account, term deposit or money market fund.
3. **Everything above that:** money you won't need for 5+ years in global index ETFs. Money you'll need sooner in bonds or deposits.

### Don't
- ❌ Lend company money to yourself without your accountant's advice (it may be taxed as a hidden distribution).
- ❌ Buy things for personal use "as investments" (art, a car you drive privately…). That's a fringe benefit.
- ⚠️ Lots of small trades raise the accountant's bill. Buy a few funds and hold them.
- ⚠️ Crypto or individual stocks are allowed, but they need proper bookkeeping and carry more risk.

## 7c. Getting money out during the year when you need it

Situation: you pay yourself the minimum fee monthly, and the rest builds up in Munbuh. What if you need extra money mid-year?

| # | Option | How fast | You keep | Limits and notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Approve the annual report early** | Dividends from **Feb–Mar** instead of July | 78% | The 30 June deadline is a *latest* date. A one-person OÜ with one client can close the books in January and approve the report straight away. **This is the biggest fix: the lock-up becomes ~1–2 months after year end.** |
| 2 | **Dividends from earlier years' profit** | Any month | 78% | Only if Munbuh already has retained profit in an approved annual report. Can be split into monthly instalments. |
| 3 | **One-off or temporarily higher board fee** | Same month (decide, pay, TSD by the 10th) | ~57% (above €700) | Always available. More expensive than dividends, but it also adds to your pension. Use for real emergencies. |
| 4 | **Reimbursement of business costs you paid personally** | Same day | **100%** | Only genuine business costs with receipts (e.g. you bought the laptop on your own card). Not income, so not taxed. |
| 5 | **Home-office rent** | Monthly | 78% | Limited to a fair rent for the office area (~€180–300/month). |
| 6 | **Loan from Munbuh to you** | Same day | 100% now, but it must be repaid | Written loan agreement, market interest (paid to Munbuh), repayment schedule. A common pattern is to repay it later with the net dividend after the annual report. Risky if it looks like a disguised dividend (never repaid, no interest, repeated): it could then be taxed at 22/78. **Only with your accountant's approval.** |
| 7 | **Personal emergency fund** | Instant | 100% | Keep 2–3 months of personal costs in your own account, so options 3 and 6 are rarely needed. |

### Recommended setup
1. Pay yourself a fee you can actually live on (see §3b, e.g. €2,000–2,500 net) instead of the bare minimum. Then extra cash is rarely needed.
2. Ask your accountant to **close the year in January** and approve the annual report early, so dividends can be paid from ~February.
3. Keep a personal 2–3 month emergency fund.
4. If something unexpected happens: first reimburse any business costs you paid yourself, then pay a one-off board fee. A loan from Munbuh is the last resort, and only with your accountant.

### Timeline with early annual report

```
2027  Jan ─────────────────────────────── Dec   contract income builds up in Munbuh
                                                (monthly: board fee + home-office rent)
2028  Jan: books closed → Feb: annual report approved → Feb/Mar: 2027 dividends
      (lump sum, or monthly instalments for the rest of 2028)
```

## 8b. Minimum salary and what Munbuh can pay for

### Minimum board fee for health insurance and pension (2026)

| Figure (2026) | Amount | What it's for |
|---|---|---|
| **Minimum monthly social-tax base** | **€886** | A board member is health-insured when social tax is paid on at least this amount. **This is your minimum fee.** |
| Minimum wage (employment contracts) | €886 Jan–Mar, **€946 from 1 April 2026** | Only binding for employment contracts, not board fees |

**€886 board fee, from company cost to net:**

| Step | Formula | Monthly | % of company cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cost to Munbuh** | €886 × 1.33 | **€1,178.38** | 100% |
| − Social tax, pension (20%) | €886 × 20% | −€177.20 | 15.0% |
| − Social tax, health (13%) | €886 × 13% | −€115.18 | 9.8% |
| **= Gross fee** | | **€886.00** | 75.2% |
| − II pillar 2% | €886 × 2% | −€17.72 | 1.5% |
| − Income tax | (€886 − €17.72 − €700) × 22% | −€37.02 | 3.1% |
| **= Net to you** | | **€831.26** | 70.5% |

- It covers **health insurance + state pension (I pillar) + II pillar**. It does not cover unemployment insurance.
- **2027:** the base is set in the state budget (probably around €946). Raise the fee in January if it changes, otherwise coverage can lapse.
- Pay it **every month**. Health insurance for board members depends on the social tax being paid.

### What Munbuh can and can't pay for

| Expense | Can Munbuh pay it? | Tax |
|---|---|---|
| Coworking desk or office rented from a third party | ✅ Yes | None, fully deductible |
| **Part of your home as an office**, through a written lease from you to Munbuh (an area used only for work, at a market price) | ✅ Yes | Munbuh deducts it. **You declare it as rental income and pay 22% income tax** (no social tax), so you keep 78%, monthly, without waiting for the annual report |
| Your whole apartment rent, or rent for a space you also live in | ❌ No | Fringe benefit, ~41% |
| Home internet or electricity, documented business share | ⚠️ Business share only, agree the method with your accountant | None on the business share |
| Laptop, monitor, phone used for work, software, books, courses, conferences | ✅ Yes | None |
| **Coffee or lunch with a client or business partner** | ✅ Yes, as hospitality expenses | Tax-free up to **€50/month + 2% of that month's social-taxed payments** (e.g. +€17.72 with an €886 fee). Above that: 22/78 |
| **Coffee alone at a café** | ❌ No, it's personal | Fringe benefit, ~41% |
| Sports or health costs (gym, massage, sports events) | ✅ Yes | Tax-free up to **€400/year** (board members count; confirm with EMTA) |
| Work trips (transport, hotel, daily allowance abroad) | ✅ Yes, with a travel order | None within EMTA limits |
| Groceries, clothes, personal rent, holidays | ❌ No | Pay from **salary or dividends** |

**Rule of thumb:** if it would exist without the business, it's personal. Pay it from your salary or dividends.

## 8c. Home-office rent: how the "78%" works

**Idea:** you (a private person) rent part of your home, for example a room used only as an office, to **Munbuh**. Munbuh pays you rent every month. Rent isn't salary, so **no social tax and no II pillar**. Only **22% income tax** applies, so you keep 78%.

### Example: Munbuh pays you €300/month rent

```
Munbuh bank account
   │  €300 rent (business expense, reduces Munbuh's profit, so no CIT on it)
   ▼
 − €66   income tax 22% (withheld by Munbuh and paid via TSD,
          or declared on your personal return; your accountant sets it up)
   ▼
 €234 in your personal account  = 78%
```

| | Amount | % |
|---|---|---|
| Rent paid by Munbuh | €300.00 | 100% |
| − Income tax 22% | −€66.00 | 22% |
| **= You receive** | **€234.00** | **78%** |

There's no tax-free €700 allowance here, because your board fee (≥ €886) already uses it up.

### Same €300 of company money, three routes

| Route | You get | % | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Home-office rent** | €234.00 | **78%** | **Monthly**, from day one |
| Dividend | €234.00 | 78% | Only after the annual report is approved |
| Extra board fee | €172.40 (€225.56 gross − 2% − 22%) | 57% | Monthly |

So rent gives you **dividend-level tax with salary-like timing**. That's why it helps in 2027, before dividends are allowed.

### Example: you rent your apartment for €1,000/month

| Step | Who | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| You pay your landlord, as always, from your personal account | You → landlord | −€1,000 |
| You sublet a 12 m² room (20% of a 60 m² apartment) to Munbuh as an office | Lease: you → Munbuh | |
| Munbuh pays you rent for that room (20% of €1,000) | Munbuh → you | +€200 |
| Income tax 22% on that rent | You → state | −€44 |
| **You keep** | | **+€156** |
| **Your real personal housing cost** | | **€1,000 − €156 = €844** |

- The €200 is company money. Munbuh records it as a business cost (office rent).
- Tax-wise it's **the same as a €200 dividend** (both leave you €156). The gain is **timing**: it arrives monthly from day one. It's also cheaper than paying the €200 as salary, which would leave ~€115.
- **Needs your landlord's written consent to sublet** (Law of Obligations Act).
- If you don't need extra monthly cash, skip it. It's optional.

### Rules to make it legitimate
1. **Written lease** between you and Munbuh (area, purpose, price, start date).
2. **A real, exclusive work space:** a room or clearly separated area used for work only.
3. **A market-level price for that area only.** Example: a 12 m² room in a 60 m² apartment = 20% of the space, and at ~€15/m² that's about €180–300/month. You can't call your whole income "rent".
4. **Paid by bank transfer** monthly, with the accountant booking it as rent and handling the income tax.
5. **If you rent your home yourself:** subletting usually needs **your landlord's consent**. You still pay 22% on the rent Munbuh pays you, even though you pay rent to your landlord (you can't deduct your own rent).
6. **If you own the home:** the business-use share could affect the tax exemption when you sell your residence. Ask your accountant before setting it up.

### Size of the benefit
It's a **supplement, not a salary**. At about €250/month it's €3,000/year, with roughly €700/year saved compared with paying the same amount as board fee. It's useful for bridging 2027, but your living money still mainly comes from the board fee and, later, dividends.

> Possible extra: Estonian law lets private individuals renting out **residential** property be taxed on 80% of the rent (a 20% deduction), which would mean keeping ~82%. Whether it applies to a room rented to your own company as an office is uncertain, so ask your accountant. The 78% above assumes it doesn't.

## 9. Caveats
- The €700 allowance is per **person**, not per income source. If you have other taxable income (for example Töötukassa benefit in the same year), it's shared, and the annual tax return settles the difference.
- The 2027 minimum social-tax base (€946) is an assumption. Check the 2027 state budget figure in December.
- Rules on loans from the OÜ to you, interim dividends and company cars are nuanced. Confirm with your accountant before using them.
- Not tax advice. Verify with an Estonian accountant, especially the 2027 cash-flow plan.
